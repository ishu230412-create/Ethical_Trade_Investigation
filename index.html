Issue

How did the animal testing become so common for testing cosmetics?

Animal testing became a really common in the 1930s after several tragic consumers injuries and side effects that were caused. One of the major example is The Lash Lure Disaster (1933): An eyelash dye named Lash Lure blinded 12 women and severely injured others because it contained an untested toxic chemical. After incidents like these, many countries passed a law that suggests all brands need to prove that their products are safe for human use before they are released.

Why are animals used in testing cosmetics?

Animals are used in cosmetic testing to check if the new products and ingredients are safe for human use. Many companies Test the items on animals to see if it causes an irritation, eye damage or any other side effect. However, these often causes a lot of pain to the animals.




What effects does it have on the animals?

Testing cosmetics products on animals causes severe pain, stress, blindness, burns, irritation, distress and long-term effects. Some of the test done involve use of toxic chemicals that may damage the organs and often to deaths of millions of animals per year.




How does animal testing impact the environment?

Animal testing produces large amount of biological and chemical waste that needs to be disposed of safely. The facilities require a large amount of water, energy and other resources to operate the tests. The toxic materials that are used in the experiment can harm the environment if they are not handled properly.

How does the labelling of the products effect the choices of the consumers?

The labelling of a product can have a strong influence on cosmetics consumers by acting as a powerful tool for safety, personal values, and visual support. For many consumers, the ingredients list is a crucial safety guide which helps the ones with sensitive skin. Beyond that, labels also play an important role in the decisions made by a shopper. Clear callouts like “cruelty-free”, “Vegan” or etc draw immediate attention of the buyer. Finally comes the visual appearance of the label. The visual design plays a major psychological role that says “We first eat with our eyes”. Labelling using specific colours, typography, spacing, and elements which captures the attention even when placed on a crowded shelf.



Better Alternatives

Are there other ways of testing cosmetics without using animals?

Yes, many modern techniques have been discovered that do not involve animal testing.





How people in Australia can influence the issue?

People can influence the issue of cosmetic animal testing through their everyday choices and activities. By using cruelty free products, users send a clear message to companies that animal lives matter. This encourages the companies to stop testing products on animals. Australians can also support campaigns, protests, and organisations that push the government to enforces stronger laws against animal testing. The public can speak up through social media, school projects, community decision which will help in spreading awareness and put pressure on the companies.



How and why that situation has developed

The situations have developed because of the early safety laws that were implemented in 1930s-1950s which suggests that a company needs to prove that their product is safe to use on humans and causes no side effects. Animal testing was the easiest option, but as the industry grew these method started to become routine and companies kept using them to keep records and data. Over time, international trading became a problem as countries like China needed to test the imported product before they were placed on the shelves, forcing the global brand to continue the practices. In the previous few years modern science has found alternatives (Given Above) to animal testing and many countries have put a ban on it but there are still many that haven’t.


Taking Action

Who is affected and how by the current situation of animal testing locally and overseas?

Animal testing effects animals, companies, costumers and the government around the earth. The animals experience a lot pain, stress, injuries, organ damage and many times even death. In many places consumers are affected by the product prices, limited choices in products and confusion that is caused by unclear labelling. Companies face higher costs, strict regulations and the pressure that is being put on them to switch to cruelty free products. The government must balance safety laws, requirements and costumer demands.



How will the changes in the testing of products on animals affect the future?

The change from traditional animal testing methods to modern, humanized technologies will make the testing much safer, faster and cheaper. According to the new laws and bans in many countries, companies have started to replace animal testing with advanced tools (As given in Better alternatives). These new methods are also much more accurate and reliable as they use human data rather than animal biology. In the upcoming generations this change the side effects that were caused in animal testing such as they are cost efficient, and help to get the safe products to the market for the consumers to buy.
